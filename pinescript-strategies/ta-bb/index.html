What is ta.bb in Pine Script?

ta.bb is a built-in function in Pine Script that calculates Bollinger Bands, one of the most widely used technical indicators in financial markets. Developed by John Bollinger in the 1980s, Bollinger Bands consist of a middle band (typically a simple moving average) and two outer bands that represent standard deviations above and below the middle band. The distance between the bands dynamically adjusts based on market volatility, making them a powerful tool for identifying overbought and oversold conditions, volatility breaks, and trend reversals.

The key innovation of Bollinger Bands is their ability to adapt to changing market volatility. When volatility increases, the bands widen; when volatility decreases, the bands narrow. This dynamic nature makes Bollinger Bands particularly valuable for detecting periods of low volatility (often followed by significant price movements) and identifying price levels where an asset may be overextended. Price touching the upper or lower band doesn't necessarily signal a reversal, but it does indicate that the price is relatively high or low compared to recent history.

Bollinger Bands are versatile and can be applied across all timeframes and asset classes. They are commonly used for identifying potential reversal points, confirming trends, and detecting breakout opportunities. The combination of a moving average with volatility-based bands provides traders with a comprehensive framework for understanding price behavior and market dynamics.

  • ▸Basis (Middle Band): A simple moving average that represents the central trend.
  • ▸Upper Band: Basis + (Standard Deviation * Multiplier). Acts as dynamic resistance.
  • ▸Lower Band: Basis - (Standard Deviation * Multiplier). Acts as dynamic support.
  • ▸Volatility Measurement: Band width indicates the current level of volatility.
  • ▸Dynamic Adaptation: Bands automatically widen or narrow based on volatility.
02

ta.bb Function Signature and Return Values

pine-script@terminal
[basis, upper, lower] = ta.bb(close, length, mult)

// Parameters:
// close  - Series of close prices
// length - The lookback period for SMA and standard deviation (default: 20)
// mult   - The multiplier for standard deviation (default: 2.0)

// Returns:
// [basis, upper, lower] - Tuple containing:
//   basis: The middle band (SMA of close over 'length' periods)
//   upper: The upper band (basis + mult * standard deviation)
//   lower: The lower band (basis - mult * standard deviation)

// Mathematical Representation:
// basis = SMA(close, length)
// stdDev = Standard Deviation(close, length)
// upper = basis + (mult * stdDev)
// lower = basis - (mult * stdDev)

// Common Defaults:
// length = 20, mult = 2.0
// These settings capture approximately 95% of price action

Understanding Bollinger Band Components and Interpretation

Each component of Bollinger Bands provides unique insights into market conditions:

  • ▸Basis (Middle Band): The SMA serves as the central trend line. When price is above the basis, it suggests an uptrend; when below, a downtrend. The slope of the basis indicates trend direction and strength.
  • ▸Upper Band: Acts as dynamic resistance. When price touches or exceeds the upper band, it may indicate overbought conditions, but in strong trends, price can ride the upper band for extended periods.
  • ▸Lower Band: Acts as dynamic support. When price touches or falls below the lower band, it may indicate oversold conditions, but in strong downtrends, price can hug the lower band.
  • ▸Band Width: The distance between the upper and lower bands. Narrow bands indicate low volatility (squeeze), while wide bands indicate high volatility.
  • ▸Percent B (%B): The position of price relative to the bands: %B = (Close - Lower) / (Upper - Lower). Values above 1 indicate price above the upper band; below 0 indicate price below the lower band.
  • ▸Bollinger Squeeze: When the bands narrow significantly, it indicates a period of low volatility that often precedes a significant price move.

Bollinger Squeeze Detection

pine-script@terminal
//@version=5
indicator("Bollinger Squeeze Detection", overlay=true)

// Input parameters
length = input.int(20, title="BB Length", minval=1)
mult = input.float(2.0, title="BB Multiplier", minval=0.1, step=0.1)
squeezeThreshold = input.float(0.1, title="Squeeze Threshold", minval=0.01, step=0.01)

// Calculate Bollinger Bands
[basis, upper, lower] = ta.bb(close, length, mult)

// Calculate band width
bandWidth = (upper - lower) / basis

// Detect squeeze
isSqueeze = bandWidth < squeezeThreshold

// Detect squeeze release
squeezeRelease = isSqueeze and not isSqueeze[1]

// Plot squeeze indicator
plotshape(squeezeRelease, title="Squeeze Release", location=location.belowbar, color=color.green, style=shape.labelup, text="SQUEEZE")

// Color bars during squeeze
barcolor(isSqueeze ? color.new(color.yellow, 60) : na)

// Plot band width in separate pane
plot(bandWidth, title="Band Width", color=color.purple, linewidth=2, display=display.pane)
hline(squeezeThreshold, "Squeeze Threshold", color=color.orange, linestyle=hline.style_dashed, display=display.pane)

Practical ta.bb Trading Strategies

1. Bollinger Bands Mean Reversion Strategy

This strategy enters trades when price reaches the outer bands and shows reversal signs.

//@version=5
strategy("BB Mean Reversion", overlay=true, initial_capital=10000)

// Input parameters
length = input.int(20, title="BB Length", minval=1)
mult = input.float(2.0, title="BB Multiplier", minval=0.1, step=0.1)

// Calculate Bollinger Bands
[basis, upper, lower] = ta.bb(close, length, mult)

// Entry signals
longCondition = close < lower and close > open
shortCondition = close > upper and close < open

// Exit signals
exitLong = close > basis
exitShort = close < basis

// Execute trades
if (longCondition)
    strategy.entry("Long", strategy.long)

if (shortCondition)
    strategy.entry("Short", strategy.short)

if (exitLong)
    strategy.close("Long")

if (exitShort)
    strategy.close("Short")

// Plot signals
plotshape(longCondition, title="Buy Signal", location=location.belowbar, color=color.green, style=shape.labelup, text="BUY")
plotshape(shortCondition, title="Sell Signal", location=location.abovebar, color=color.red, style=shape.labeldown, text="SELL")

// Plot Bollinger Bands
plot(basis, title="Basis", color=color.blue, linewidth=2)
plot(upper, title="Upper", color=color.green, linewidth=2)
plot(lower, title="Lower", color=color.red, linewidth=2)

2. Bollinger Squeeze Breakout Strategy

This strategy enters trades when price breaks out of a squeeze.

//@version=5
strategy("BB Squeeze Breakout", overlay=true, initial_capital=10000)

// Input parameters
length = input.int(20, title="BB Length", minval=1)
mult = input.float(2.0, title="BB Multiplier", minval=0.1, step=0.1)
squeezeThreshold = input.float(0.1, title="Squeeze Threshold", minval=0.01, step=0.01)

// Calculate Bollinger Bands
[basis, upper, lower] = ta.bb(close, length, mult)

// Calculate band width
bandWidth = (upper - lower) / basis
isSqueeze = bandWidth < squeezeThreshold

// Entry signals - breakout after squeeze
breakoutLong = isSqueeze and not isSqueeze[1] and close > upper
breakoutShort = isSqueeze and not isSqueeze[1] and close < lower

if (breakoutLong)
    strategy.entry("Breakout Long", strategy.long)

if (breakoutShort)
    strategy.entry("Breakout Short", strategy.short)

// Exit when bands widen significantly
strategy.close("Breakout Long", when=bandWidth > squeezeThreshold * 3)
strategy.close("Breakout Short", when=bandWidth > squeezeThreshold * 3)

// Plot signals
plotshape(breakoutLong, title="Breakout Buy", location=location.belowbar, color=color.green, style=shape.labelup, text="BO")
plotshape(breakoutShort, title="Breakout Sell", location=location.abovebar, color=color.red, style=shape.labeldown, text="BR")

// Plot bands
plot(basis, title="Basis", color=color.blue, linewidth=2)
plot(upper, title="Upper", color=color.green, linewidth=2)
plot(lower, title="Lower", color=color.red, linewidth=2)

// Highlight squeeze periods
fill(plot(upper), plot(lower), color=isSqueeze ? color.new(color.yellow, 30) : color.new(color.purple, 10))

3. BB with Trend Confirmation Strategy

This strategy combines Bollinger Bands with a trend indicator for higher probability trades.

//@version=5
strategy("BB with Trend Confirmation", overlay=true, initial_capital=10000)

// Input parameters
bbLength = input.int(20, title="BB Length", minval=1)
bbMult = input.float(2.0, title="BB Multiplier", minval=0.1, step=0.1)
maLength = input.int(50, title="Trend MA Length", minval=1)

// Calculate Bollinger Bands
[basis, upper, lower] = ta.bb(close, bbLength, bbMult)

// Trend indicator
trendMA = ta.sma(close, maLength)
isUptrend = close > trendMA
isDowntrend = close < trendMA

// Entry signals with trend confirmation
longCondition = isUptrend and close < lower and close > open
shortCondition = isDowntrend and close > upper and close < open

// Exit signals
strategy.close("Long", when=close > basis or not isUptrend)
strategy.close("Short", when=close < basis or not isDowntrend)

if (longCondition)
    strategy.entry("Long", strategy.long)

if (shortCondition)
    strategy.entry("Short", strategy.short)

// Plot indicators
plot(trendMA, title="Trend MA", color=color.orange, linewidth=2)
plot(basis, title="Basis", color=color.blue, linewidth=2)
plot(upper, title="Upper", color=color.green, linewidth=2)
plot(lower, title="Lower", color=color.red, linewidth=2)

// Plot signals
plotshape(longCondition, title="Buy Signal", location=location.belowbar, color=color.green, style=shape.labelup, text="BUY")
plotshape(shortCondition, title="Sell Signal", location=location.abovebar, color=color.red, style=shape.labeldown, text="SELL")

Key ta.bb Parameters and Optimization

Key ta.bb Parameters and Optimization

length (20): The lookback period for SMA and standard deviation. Standard setting that works across most markets. Shorter periods increase sensitivity, longer periods reduce noise.
mult (2.0): The standard deviation multiplier. Captures approximately 95% of price action. Higher values widen bands, lower values narrow them.
basis: The middle SMA that represents the central trend.
upper/lower: The volatility-based support and resistance levels.
Band Width: A measure of volatility that can be used for squeeze detection and volatility analysis.

08

Optimizing ta.bb for Better Performance

To get the most out of ta.bb in your Pine Script strategies, consider the following optimization techniques:

  • ▸Adjust Length for Timeframe: For shorter timeframes (1-minute, 5-minute), consider using shorter lengths (10-15). For longer timeframes (daily, weekly), use longer lengths (20-30).
  • ▸Adjust Multiplier for Market: In volatile markets, consider using 2.5 or 3.0 to avoid excessive band touches. In stable markets, 1.5-2.0 may provide more signals.
  • ▸Use Multiple Timeframes: Check Bollinger Bands on higher timeframes for trend context and lower timeframes for entry timing.
  • ▸Combine with Other Indicators: Use Bollinger Bands with RSI, MACD, or volume indicators for signal confirmation.
  • ▸Dynamic Parameters: Consider adjusting the multiplier based on market volatility using ATR or other volatility measurements.
  • ▸Percent B (%B): Use %B to identify overbought (above 1) and oversold (below 0) conditions more precisely.
09

ta.bb vs Keltner Channels

Understanding the differences between Bollinger Bands and Keltner Channels can help you choose the right tool for your strategy:

  • ▸Calculation Basis: Bollinger Bands use standard deviation for band width, while Keltner Channels use Average True Range (ATR). This makes Bollinger Bands more responsive to volatility spikes.
  • ▸Volatility Measurement: Bollinger Bands expand and contract based on standard deviation, which reacts quickly to volatility. Keltner Channels use ATR, which provides smoother band movements.
  • ▸Price Touch Frequency: Bollinger Bands capture approximately 95% of price action with a 2.0 multiplier. Keltner Channels typically capture about 85-90% of price action.
  • ▸Signal Generation: Bollinger Bands are better for identifying volatility breaks and squeezes. Keltner Channels are better for trend following and mean reversion strategies.
  • ▸Market Context: Bollinger Bands work well in all market conditions but are particularly effective in ranging markets. Keltner Channels are often preferred in trending markets.
  • ▸Combined Use: Some traders use both indicators together, with Bollinger Bands for volatility analysis and Keltner Channels for trend identification.

Pro Tip: Combining Bollinger Bands with RSI

Pro Tip: Combining Bollinger Bands with RSI

One of the most powerful combinations is Bollinger Bands with RSI. When price touches the lower band and RSI is below 30, it signals a strong oversold condition with high probability of reversal. Conversely, when price touches the upper band and RSI is above 70, it signals a strong overbought condition. This combination significantly improves the accuracy of mean reversion trades.

11

Common ta.bb Pitfalls to Avoid

While Bollinger Bands are a powerful indicator, there are several common mistakes that traders should avoid:

  • ▸Assuming Band Touch = Reversal: Price touching the upper or lower band does not automatically mean a reversal. In strong trends, price can ride the bands for extended periods.
  • ▸Using Default Parameters Blindly: The 20/2 settings work well for many markets, but they may need adjustment for specific assets or timeframes.
  • ▸Ignoring Trend Context: Always consider the broader trend. In strong trends, mean reversion strategies at the bands are less effective.
  • ▸Over-relying on Squeeze Detection: A squeeze doesn't guarantee a breakout direction. Always wait for price confirmation before entering trades.
  • ▸Using on Illiquid Markets: Bollinger Bands are less effective on markets with thin trading volume or wide bid-ask spreads.
  • ▸Not Using Confirmation: Always confirm band signals with other indicators or price action patterns for higher probability trades.
  • ▸Ignoring %B: %B provides additional context beyond simple band touches and can help identify overbought/oversold conditions more precisely.
