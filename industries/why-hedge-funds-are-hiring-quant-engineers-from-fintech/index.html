Why Hedge Funds Hire Quant Engineers from Fintech

Hedge funds need quant engineers who can build real-time trading systems, market data pipelines, and execution infrastructure. Fintech engineers bring modern C++ and Rust skills, real-time system expertise, and a product-focused mindset. This guide explains why hedge funds are increasingly hiring from fintech.

Trading systems must handle millions of messages per second. Market data requires nanosecond timestamps and replayability. Order execution must be reliable with automatic failover. Risk management requires real-time position calculation.

Common Mistakes When Hiring Hedge Fund Quant Engineers

Hiring fintech engineers without low-latency experience

Why it happens: Fintech has different latency requirements

Impact: Systems too slow for HFT strategies

💡Test low-latency C++ or Rust specifically

Ignoring real-time risk requirements

Why it happens: Focus on execution, not risk

Impact: Slow risk calculations expose firm to losses

💡Design real-time risk from day one

Not testing market data knowledge

Why it happens: Data feeds are complex

Impact: Market data gaps and inconsistencies

💡Test feed handler design specifically

Underestimating compliance requirements

Why it happens: Fintech has lighter regulation

Impact: Failed audits and regulatory fines

💡Design audit trails from day one

Hiring without failover experience

Why it happens: Single system seems sufficient

Impact: Downtime during failures

💡Design redundant systems with automatic failover

Frequently Asked Questions About Hedge Fund Quant Engineering

C++ vs Rust for hedge fund systems?
Both used. C++ dominates legacy systems. Rust is gaining for new systems due to memory safety without performance loss. Choose based on team expertise.
What's the compensation for hedge fund quant engineers?
$250k-600k+ total compensation. Top engineers earn more through P&L sharing.
How long to build a trading platform?
12-24 months for production-ready platforms. Start with MVP for a single strategy, then expand.
